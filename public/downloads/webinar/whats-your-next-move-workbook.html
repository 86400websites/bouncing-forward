A note from Maher

Thank you for giving an hour to this. I know what an hour costs when your plans have just come apart.

Bouncing Forward began with the worst day of my life. In January 2008 my son Hikmat was seventeen when a car struck him in Amman and the driver drove on. For a long time there was nothing beside that fact. Then a question appeared: what happens next? Over time that question changed shape into a better one, and it is the question I would like you to keep: not why is this happening to me, but what does this ask of me?

Your setback and mine are different, and I would never pretend otherwise. But the question works the same way for both. It takes your attention off what cannot be changed and puts it on the next move. Everything in the webinar, and everything in these pages, is built to help you find that move and take it.

You will notice there is no instruction here to think positively or to put it behind you. What I hope you take from this is permission: to look honestly at what has changed, to draw a line between what is settled and what is still yours, and to take one small step in a direction you have chosen.

The direction matters more than the scale. One conversation, one application, one step, repeated, is how a life changes. It is how mine did.

Go well, and keep bouncing forward.

Maher Kaddoura
Author of Bouncing Forward

The webinar in two pages

Everything we covered, in the order we covered it.

Plans change

Jobs change, relationships change, businesses change, bodies change. So the real question is less 'will my plan change?' and more 'what's my next move when it does?'

This wasn't the plan

Whatever your version, it starts the day the ground shifts. Every setback counts: the details may differ, but the feeling of the ground shifting is the same.

One event, two losses

The first loss is what happened, and everyone can see it. The second is the future you had pictured, invisible to everyone else. Simply naming it makes it lighter.

Where am I now?

Honesty is dropping the pin: one plain sentence about what changed and where you really are today, and a number from one to five for how ready you feel.

Acceptance is a bridge

What happened is on one bank and your next move is on the other. Accepting means being accurate rather than agreeing. You can dislike the river and still cross it.

Draw the Control Line

On the left, what is already settled. On the right, what is still in your hands: how you respond, who you talk to, what you try next. Circle one thing to carry forward.

What does this ask of me?

Reflection moves you along, while going round in circles brings you back to the start. When the road closes, ask what this asks of you from exactly where you are.

What matters more or less now?

What matters more now, what matters less, and what have I learned about myself? Many people grow after big changes, with clearer priorities and a stronger sense of what they can handle.

Resilience: bending is not breaking

Bamboo bends almost flat in a storm and stands straight back up when the wind drops. People adapt to big changes far better and faster than they expect.

Support: built in quiet seasons

A support net is built with every call you return and every small kindness. If yours feels thin, see that as information rather than a verdict.

A direction is enough

Leave the five-year plan for later. What you need now is a direction: one honest sentence about what you would like to be different. Maher calls it purpose before plans.

Six months from now

One concrete thing, honest rather than impressive. Small and true beats big and borrowed. Then add your reason: why does this matter to me? Because…

Optimism. The real kind.

Real optimism is quieter than a big smile. It simply keeps the door open, whether you feel hopeful on the day or not.

The 24-hour move

The smallest real step you can take within twenty-four hours. Ten minutes or less, starting with a verb: call, send, walk, book, ask. Add a day, a time and a witness.

The smallest real step

The step comes first and the plan takes shape behind it. Maher's first step was a newspaper and a piece of paper, counting the accidents on Jordan's roads.

Adaptability: keep the direction

When life answers with a twist, recalculate like a GPS. Change how you get there, and keep heading the same way.

Your road map

The path is what you do; the compass is what you draw on. Resilience, support, optimism and adaptability: four strengths you already carry into your next move.
